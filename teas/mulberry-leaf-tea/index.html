Mulberry Leaf Tea (Sang Ye Cha) — Cooling Liver Tea

🍵 Tea & Tonic · 4 min read
Quick answer

Mulberry leaf tea, known in Chinese as Sang Ye Cha (桑叶茶), is a light infusion made from mulberry leaves. In traditional Chinese food therapy, it is described as cooling, with a sweet-bitter flavor and traditional associations with the Lung and Liver systems. Its clean, grassy taste makes it a refreshing alternative to sweetened drinks in warm weather.

Mulberry Leaf in TCM

Classical materia medica traditionally describes mulberry leaf as cool in nature and sweet-bitter in flavor, entering the Lung and Liver meridians. Traditional texts use phrases such as “disperse wind-heat” and “clear the Lung.” These phrases describe a pattern-based tradition rather than a biomedical effect.

Mulberry leaf is also traditionally associated with the eyes, which explains its frequent pairing with chrysanthemum or goji berries. Persistent eye pain, vision changes, fever, breathing difficulty, or a lasting cough should be assessed by a qualified clinician. For practical lifestyle ideas, see SymptomCalm, while keeping tea in a supportive role.

What It May Support

Within a TCM food-therapy framework, a mild cup may support a refreshed feeling in hot weather or after rich, warming foods. Pure mulberry leaf tea is naturally caffeine-free, so some people enjoy it during the afternoon or evening. Its traditional eye-related associations make it a popular ingredient in blends for screen-heavy days.

These are traditional uses, not claims that the tea cures eye conditions, respiratory illness, blood-sugar problems, or any other disease. Tea works best alongside varied meals, adequate water, sleep, and appropriate medical care.

How to Brew Sang Ye Cha

Basic Mulberry Leaf Tea

  • Ingredients: 2–3 grams dried mulberry leaves (about 1–2 teaspoons) and 300 ml water.
  • Water: Use 90–95°C / 194–203°F water; let boiling water stand for one minute.
  • Method: Put leaves in an infuser, pour over the water, cover, and steep 5–8 minutes. Strain and sip warm.
  • Second infusion: Re-steep once for 3–5 minutes if the leaves still smell fresh.

Start with five minutes if you are new to the flavor. The tea should be grassy and mildly bitter, not harsh. Drink it warm or let it cool for iced tea. If desired, add a little honey after it cools; too much sugar hides the delicate flavor.

Traditional Pairings

Mulberry Leaf and Chrysanthemum

Combine 2 grams mulberry leaf with 1–2 grams dried chrysanthemum and 350 ml hot water. Steep 5–7 minutes. Chrysanthemum adds floral aroma and is traditionally associated with the Liver and eyes. Enjoy this light blend during the daytime.

Mulberry Leaf and Goji

Rinse 1 teaspoon goji berries and steep with 2 grams mulberry leaf in 350 ml hot water. Goji adds fruitiness. In TCM food culture, goji is traditionally viewed as more nourishing while mulberry leaf is lighter and cooling, creating a balanced blend.

With Green Tea

Add a small pinch of green tea after the mulberry infusion is ready. This preserves the leaf flavor and keeps caffeine moderate. Avoid it late in the day if caffeine affects sleep.

When to Drink It

One cup after breakfast or lunch is a practical starting point. Drinking it with food may feel gentler than drinking herbal tea on an empty stomach. Since TCM traditionally views mulberry leaf as cooling, people who often feel chilled, have loose stools, or find cold drinks upsetting may prefer half a cup, a shorter steep, or a warmer neutral tea.

There is no need to drink large quantities. Start with half a serving and observe how you feel over the next day. A tea routine should complement, not replace, regular meals, sleep, hydration, and professional advice.

Choosing and Storing the Leaves

Choose food-grade dried leaves from a reputable tea or herbal supplier. Look for a fresh leafy aroma and green to olive color, with no dampness, insects, mold, or musty smell. Do not gather leaves from roadsides or trees exposed to unknown sprays. Keep the leaves sealed, dry, and away from sunlight and strong kitchen odors. Discard them if damp, moldy, clumped, or unusual in smell.

Safety Notes

“Natural” does not mean suitable for everyone. If you take medicine for blood sugar, blood pressure, clotting, or another chronic condition, ask a clinician or pharmacist before making mulberry leaf tea a daily habit. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, childhood, and the period around surgery call for professional guidance. Concentrated powders, capsules, and extracts are not equivalent to a mild cup of tea.

Stop drinking it and seek advice if you develop a rash, stomach upset, dizziness, unusual sweating, or another concerning reaction. Do not use tea to delay care for vision changes, breathing trouble, fever, or persistent symptoms.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drink mulberry leaf tea every day?

Some adults enjoy one mild cup daily, but daily use is not necessary. Start small and ask a professional if you take medication or have a chronic condition.

Does it contain caffeine?

Pure mulberry leaf tea is naturally caffeine-free. A blend containing green, black, or pu-erh tea will contain caffeine.

Can I combine it with chrysanthemum or goji?

These are traditional pairings. Keep the blend mild and seek professional advice if pregnant, nursing, taking medication, or managing a health condition.

Traditional food-therapy note: TCM descriptions such as “cooling,” “Lung,” and “Liver” come from a traditional theoretical system and should not be read as biomedical treatment instructions.
