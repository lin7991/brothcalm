Steamed Pear with Rock Sugar: A Traditional TCM Dessert

🥘 Recipe · 5 min read
Quick answer

Few home desserts are as simple and comforting as a pear steamed until tender with a little rock sugar. Across Chinese households, variations of this preparation are traditionally served warm, especially when the weather turns cool or dry. In Chinese food therapy, pear is commonly described as cooling and moistening. That traditional language offers cultural context—not a medical promise: this recipe is a dessert, not a cough medicine or substitute for care.

Pear in traditional Chinese food therapy

TCM food traditions commonly describe pear as sweet and slightly sour, with a cooling nature, and associate it with the Lung and Stomach systems. Food energetics are a traditional framework for discussing how foods may fit different seasons and constitutions; they are not the same as a biomedical diagnosis. Cooking pear softens its texture and makes it easy to serve warm. Rock sugar adds sweetness and is used in many familiar home-style versions.

Historical food culture and regional practice have many variations. Some cooks add honey after steaming, while others include a few goji berries or a small piece of ginger. These are culinary choices rather than treatments. If you follow a personalized dietary plan, use the advice of your qualified practitioner.

Steamed pear with rock sugar recipe

Ingredients (one serving)

  • 1 ripe Asian pear (a firm European pear also works)
  • 1–2 teaspoons rock sugar, adjusted to taste
  • 1 tablespoon water
  • Optional: 2–3 goji berries, rinsed

Equipment

A small knife, spoon, heat-safe bowl that fits inside a steamer, and a covered steamer or pot with a rack.

Method

  1. Rinse the pear well. Slice off a small lid around the stem end and set it aside.
  2. With a spoon, remove the core and seeds, taking care not to pierce through the bottom.
  3. Add the rock sugar and water to the hollow. Add the optional goji berries, then replace the pear lid.
  4. Set the pear upright in a heat-safe bowl. Place it in a steamer over gently simmering water and cover.
  5. Steam for 25–35 minutes, checking that the water does not boil dry. It is ready when a knife slides into the flesh easily.
  6. Let it cool until comfortably warm. Spoon the softened fruit and syrup into a bowl and serve.

Cooking time depends on pear size and ripeness. For a softer result, steam a little longer; for firmer fruit, begin checking at 20 minutes. Keep the steamer lid closed as much as practical so the heat remains steady.

Variations and serving ideas

For a less-sweet bowl, reduce the rock sugar or leave it out; ripe fruit may be sweet enough on its own. A few goji berries add color and a mild fruity note. A very thin slice of ginger gives a warming contrast, though that changes the flavor and traditional food character of the dish. Honey can be stirred into the pear after cooking, once it has cooled slightly; do not put honey in the steamer if you prefer to preserve its delicate flavor.

Serve as a small dessert or a warm snack. The cooked pear can be refrigerated promptly in a covered container and eaten within a day; reheat gently until steaming hot, then cool to a comfortable serving temperature. Discard food left at room temperature for an extended period.

Choosing and preparing pears

Choose fruit that feels heavy for its size, with intact skin and no soft, bruised areas. A pear that is ripe but still holds its shape is easiest to core and steam. Wash it under running water before cutting, and use clean utensils. Rock sugar is sold in Asian grocery stores, but ordinary granulated sugar works as a culinary substitute; neither is necessary if you prefer an unsweetened version.

Frequently asked questions

Is steamed pear with rock sugar a cough remedy?

It is a traditional comfort food in some Chinese households, but it is not a proven cough treatment and should not replace medical evaluation or prescribed care.

Can I make it without rock sugar?

Yes. Use a ripe pear and steam it with a spoonful of water, or add no extra liquid if your steamer setup is suitable. The result will be less sweet.

Can I prepare it ahead?

It is best served freshly steamed, but you can refrigerate leftovers promptly in a covered container and use them within a day. Reheat thoroughly and serve warm, not scalding.

How often should I eat it?

There is no therapeutic serving schedule. Enjoy it as an occasional dessert in portions that suit your dietary needs and tolerance.
