Sesame Oil Chicken — A Warming Postpartum Dinner

🥘 Recipe · 7 min read

What Is Sesame Oil Chicken (麻油雞)?

Sesame oil chicken — 麻油鸡, ma you ji — is a Taiwanese and southern Chinese classic: chicken seared golden in dark toasted sesame oil with thin-sliced old ginger, simmered in rice wine and water until the broth turns amber, with goji berries at the end.

It is also one of the clearest everyday examples of 食疗 (shi liao), Chinese food therapy. Every ingredient is chosen for its energetic quality as much as its flavor: sesame oil warming and blood-nourishing, old ginger warming and dispersing, rice wine warming and moving, goji berries traditionally used to nourish Blood and the Liver. Together they warm the body's middle, dispel Cold, and help rebuild Blood and Qi.

Its most famous role is inside 坐月子 (zuo yue zi) — "sitting the month," the Chinese confinement period after childbirth. Because birth is believed to deplete Blood and Qi and leave the body open to Cold, a new mother is traditionally fed warm, fully cooked food for about thirty days, with sesame oil chicken as the centerpiece, eaten with rice or thin noodles and the broth drunk to the last spoonful.

The TCM Properties of Sesame Oil Chicken

In TCM theory a dish is read through its nature, flavor, and the channels it enters. Sesame oil chicken is warm, sweet-and-pungent, and works on the Spleen, Stomach, and Liver.

Warm nature, sweet and pungent flavor

Chicken is considered sweet and warm, entering the Spleen and Stomach, and is traditionally used as a gentle building food — nourishment that does not push. "Sweet" here is not about sugar; it is the flavor associated with tonifying the Spleen and Stomach, the digestive center that turns food into Qi and Blood.

The pungent notes come from the ginger and rice wine, which in TCM theory move Qi and Blood and are believed to push Cold outward from the interior, which is why the dish warms the chest and limbs, not only the mouth. See the basics of food energetics.

Why dark toasted black sesame oil matters

Ma you ji uses black sesame oil, toasted until dark and almost mahogany. In TCM theory black sesame seeds are sweet, neutral-to-warm, and enter the Liver and Kidney channels, where they are traditionally used to nourish Blood; the toasted oil keeps that quality and adds a moving, dispersing note. The Bencao Gangmu (Compendium of Materia Medica) lists black sesame among the foods that nourish and moisten — the association that made it the confinement oil.

Why old ginger matters

Old ginger (老姜) is preferred over young ginger — more pungent, less watery, more warming. It is traditionally used to warm the middle burner, disperse Cold, and settle the Stomach. The skin is left on because it is considered slightly cooling while the flesh is warming, so keeping both is the more balanced choice.

Traditional Uses Across the Year and the Lifecourse

After childbirth — zuo yue zi

This is the dish's signature use: labor is believed to consume Blood and Qi and open the channels to Cold, so the postpartum diet is deliberately warming and easy to digest, and sesame oil chicken is often the first substantial dish a new mother is given. Our postpartum food therapy guide covers the wider confinement diet.

Signs of Blood deficiency

Outside the postpartum period the dish is traditionally served for patterns TCM associates with Blood deficiency: a pale, dull complexion, fatigue, dizziness on standing, and cold hands and feet. Chicken, black sesame oil, and goji berries are all traditionally blood-nourishing. See foods for blood deficiency and warming foods for cold hands and feet.

Cold-damp patterns and winter warming

Ginger and rice wine supply the dispersing half, so the dish also suits cold-damp patterns — a heavy, achy body, joints that worsen in damp weather, internal chill. In winter many families make it simply because it is among the warmest dishes in the repertoire; see warming foods for winter.

How to Make Sesame Oil Chicken

This is the classic home formulation: one pot, about fifty minutes, serves four.

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, 1.2–1.5 kg, cut into pieces — or 6 bone-in thighs
  • 4–5 tbsp dark toasted sesame oil (black sesame oil)
  • 80–100 g old ginger, sliced thin, skin left on
  • 3 tbsp (about 150 ml) rice wine (mijiu)
  • 500 ml water
  • 10 g goji berries
  • Salt to taste
  • Optional: 1 tsp light soy sauce, or a small piece of rock sugar

Method

  1. Rinse, cut, and dry the chicken. For a cleaner broth, blanch it 2 minutes in boiling water and drain.
  2. Slice the ginger into thin coins, about 2 mm, skin on — 80–100 g.
  3. Toast the ginger. Heat the sesame oil in a heavy pot over low-to-medium heat and fry the ginger 2–3 minutes, until fragrant and the edges just curl. Do not let it brown or the oil smoke: burnt sesame oil is bitter and, in TCM, excessively heating.
  4. Sear the chicken in the ginger oil for 4–5 minutes, turning the pieces until opaque and lightly golden.
  5. Pour in the rice wine, raise the heat, and boil briskly 3–5 minutes to cook off the alcohol. Skip this step for the alcohol-free version.
  6. Add the water, return to a gentle boil, cover, lower the heat, and simmer 25–30 minutes for cut chicken (about 45 minutes for a whole bird), until tender and amber.
  7. Stir in the goji berries for the last 5 minutes; they should stay plump.
  8. Season with salt — plus a teaspoon of light soy sauce or a small piece of rock sugar if you like — and serve hot with rice or thin wheat noodles, drinking the broth.

Timing and heat notes

  • About 50 minutes total, including prep and searing.
  • Keep the heat low and even: a hard boil shreds the chicken and clouds the broth.
  • Bone-in, skin-on is traditional; the bones are what give the broth its body.

Cooking Tips and Two Variations

Two tips that make the biggest difference

  • Use a clay pot or heavy Dutch oven — even heat keeps the ginger from scorching and the chicken from toughening.
  • Season at the end and eat it the same day, while the oil is still fragrant.

Variation 1: Alcohol-free, for breastfeeding

Leave out the rice wine; add an extra 150 ml water plus two or three more ginger slices, or a splash of rice vinegar. If you do keep the wine, boil it open for the full 3–5 minutes. Many families use it for the nursing weeks, then return to the wine version.

Variation 2: Goji and jujube

Add 8–10 pitted, torn jujube dates with the water in step 6, plus a few extra goji berries. Jujubes are sweet and warming and traditionally used to nourish Blood and support the Spleen and Stomach, so this version is sweeter and more building; reduce or omit the rock sugar.

Cautions and Contraindications

Sesame oil chicken is among the most warming dishes in the Chinese home repertoire, so it does not suit everyone.

  • Heat signs. With a dry mouth, sore throat, constipation, or a red face, it may feel too heating; in TCM theory it suits cold patterns, not hot ones.
  • Alcohol and nursing. If pregnant or nursing, the rice wine must be fully cooked off or left out.
  • Sesame allergy. Sesame is a common allergen, and the dish is built around the oil.
  • Blood thinners. Black sesame, ginger, and goji berries carry traditional blood-moving qualities; ask your doctor first.
  • Rich and fatty. Skim some oil from the surface if you are watching your intake.
  • This is a traditional home dish, not medical care.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is sesame oil chicken in TCM?

Sesame oil chicken — 麻油雞, ma you ji — is a Taiwanese and southern Chinese braise of chicken, dark toasted black sesame oil, old ginger, and rice wine. In TCM theory it is warm in nature, sweet and pungent in flavor, and enters the Spleen, Stomach, and Liver channels. It is traditionally used to warm the middle burner, dispel Cold, and nourish Blood and Qi.

Why is sesame oil chicken traditionally eaten after childbirth?

In the Chinese confinement tradition known as zuo yue zi (坐月子), the month after birth is a time to rebuild Blood and Qi and protect the body from Cold. Sesame oil chicken is believed to be ideal for that: chicken is considered a gentle building food, black sesame oil is used in TCM theory to nourish Blood, and ginger and rice wine warm the body and disperse Cold.

Can I make sesame oil chicken without alcohol?

Yes. Leave out the rice wine and add an extra 150 ml water plus two or three more slices of ginger, or use a splash of rice vinegar. If you do use rice wine, boil it uncovered for 3 to 5 minutes so the alcohol cooks off. A goji and jujube version is also traditional for variety.

Is sesame oil chicken too heating to eat in summer?

It can be, for some people. Sesame oil chicken is a very warming dish, and in TCM theory it suits cold patterns — cold hands and feet, a pale complexion, fatigue, internal chill. If you tend toward heat signs such as a dry mouth, sore throat, constipation, or a red face, it may feel too heating, especially in hot weather. In summer, many families reduce the ginger a little.
