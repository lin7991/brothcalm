Three Treasures Breakfast Congee — Goji, Jujube and Longan

🥘 Recipe · 6 min read
Quick answer

Choose this mildly sweet congee when you want a soft, warm breakfast with a little fruitiness and very little morning prep. Rice makes the base creamy; goji, jujube and longan add chew, color and sweetness. In TCM food tradition, the combination is often described as gently nourishing, but it is food—not a treatment or a guaranteed way to change energy, blood or sleep.

This bowl is especially useful for the decision many home cooks face: should breakfast be a plain, savory congee, a sweet fruit-and-grain bowl, or something more substantial? Three Treasures Congee sits between those options. It is comforting and portable, yet easy to adapt if dried fruit feels too sweet. The recipe below makes four modest servings, so you can test one bowl before buying a large bag of ingredients.

Is this guide for you?

  • Try it if: you like soft breakfasts, need a make-ahead option, or want a gentle way to use goji, jujube and longan.
  • Choose another guide if: you want a savory, higher-protein meal; start with basic congee and add egg, tofu or chicken.
  • Keep it occasional: if sweet dried fruit bothers your digestion or you are watching concentrated sugars, use the low-sweetness variation below.

The TCM lens: a traditional food map

In TCM food therapy, white rice is commonly described as sweet and neutral, associated traditionally with the Spleen and Stomach systems. Goji is traditionally considered sweet and neutral, jujube sweet and warm, and longan sweet and warm. Practitioners may discuss these foods in terms of supporting qi or blood, nourishing yin, or reducing a sense of dryness. These are traditional frameworks, not anatomy, laboratory diagnoses or proven treatment claims.

The same lens warns against assuming that “more nourishing” is always better. A very sweet, rich bowl may feel heavy for someone who already dislikes greasy foods or tends toward a damp, sluggish sensation in TCM language. Think of the theory as a way to personalize flavor, temperature and portion—not as a label for your medical condition.

The modern food lens

Rice supplies carbohydrate; the dried fruits provide sweetness, small amounts of fiber and micronutrients. Because drying concentrates fruit, a handful contains more sugar by weight than the same volume of fresh fruit. The recipe is also low in protein unless you add milk, yogurt, egg, tofu or nuts. Nutrition details vary with rice type, portion and brand, so treat this as a comforting grain dish rather than a complete nutrition prescription. Research on individual berries or extracts does not automatically prove that this home recipe produces a particular health outcome.

Try it once: Three Treasures Congee

Ingredients — 4 small servings

  • 100 g (½ cup) white jasmine or short-grain rice
  • 1.2 litres (5 cups) water or unsalted broth
  • 6 dried goji berries, rinsed
  • 2 dried jujubes, split and pitted
  • 2 teaspoons dried longan, rinsed
  • 1 thin slice fresh ginger, optional
  • Pinch of salt; toasted sesame seeds or milk to finish, optional

Method

  1. Rinse the rice until the water is mostly clear. Soak for 20 minutes if you have time; it shortens cooking.
  2. Put rice, water, jujube and ginger in a heavy pot. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a very low simmer.
  3. Cook uncovered for 45–60 minutes, stirring every 10 minutes near the end, until the grains break down and the surface looks silky.
  4. Stir in goji and longan during the final 10 minutes. Remove the ginger, add a pinch of salt, and cool briefly before serving.

Timing and swaps: Eat one small bowl warm at breakfast or as an afternoon snack. Refrigerate promptly and use within three days; loosen with water when reheating. For less sweetness, use one jujube and one teaspoon longan. For a savory bowl, omit longan, add scallions and top with a softly cooked egg. For a dairy-free creamy finish, stir in unsweetened soy milk after cooking, not while boiling.

Troubleshooting and personalization

  • Too sweet: dilute with hot water, add salt and ginger, or repurpose tomorrow’s portion as a savory base.
  • Too thin: simmer uncovered another 10 minutes. Too thick: add hot water gradually; congee thickens as it cools.
  • Fruit feels tough: soak jujube and longan for 10 minutes before cooking, and check for pits.
  • You need more staying power: add an egg, tofu or a spoon of nut butter, provided those foods suit you.

Specific safety boundaries

Do not use this recipe to manage low blood sugar, anemia, fatigue, digestive symptoms or any diagnosed condition. Dried fruit can contribute meaningful carbohydrate; people with diabetes or medication-related glucose concerns should ask their clinician about portions. Avoid whole jujube pits, check packaged ingredients for allergens, and stop if you notice an adverse reaction. If pregnant, nursing, taking regular medicines, or managing kidney, liver or other chronic conditions, discuss concentrated dried herbs or frequent use with a qualified professional. Food amounts here are culinary guidance, not a medicinal dose.
